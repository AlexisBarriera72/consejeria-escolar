## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment.** The homepage is the least product-specific surface in this product, which is remarkable given what surrounds it. One level down, `/guias` renders a genuine filing-cabinet tab — a folder shoulder built from asymmetric border radii — plus a search field and person-chips. `/consejered` renders a hallway photo wall: four cards rotated between -1.6° and 1.5°, straightening on hover, each with a pushpin dot, a per-person accent border and a room location. Those pages are unmistakably this product.

The homepage, sitting between them, is a centered `<h1>`, a mascot, and a three-up grid of white `rounded-2xl` cards with a coloured band on top — the single most transplantable layout in web design. Swap three nouns and it is a dentist, a bootcamp, a SaaS pricing page. Nothing on it is tilted, pinned, tabbed, corked or papered. The "La Oficina" vocabulary that the rest of the site speaks fluently is entirely absent from the room where visitors arrive.

What is genuinely authored here: the Puerto Rico vocabulary, the accent-per-section system, the decision to show real content in the previews rather than blurbs, and the avatar/bubble mechanism. But that mechanism spends its one interesting interaction explaining three labels that need no explaining, while the things that would need explaining — what this office does, when she is actually there, what happens if you walk in — go unsaid.

**Deterministic scan.** `detect.mjs` over the homepage surface: **exit 0, zero findings**. Over the whole `app`/`components` tree: exit 2, three findings, all rule `side-tab`, all `warning`:

- `app/consejered/[slug]/page.tsx:115` — `border-l-4` on a pull-quote. **True positive.**
- `components/panel/FormularioAcceso.tsx:51` — `border-l-4` on an error callout. **True positive.**
- `components/ReproductorVideo.tsx:79` — `border-l-16`. **False positive**: it is the CSS-triangle play glyph, `border-y-10 border-y-transparent` composing an arrow inside a round button. The regex matched the left border without seeing the transparent Y-borders that make it a shape.

Custom-property resolution, selector matching and computed contrast were therefore never evaluated for any homepage file. There is also no `DESIGN.md` and no `.impeccable/config.json`, so zero design-system conformance rules ran. Read the homepage's clean result as "clean for the regex-tier rules that executed," not a clean bill of health.

**Visual overlays.** None. There is no browser-automation tool in this session, so no script injection was attempted and **no user-visible overlay exists** — do not go looking for one in a browser tab. Fallback signal used instead: both agents inspected the real server-rendered HTML over HTTP from the running dev server, plus independently recomputed contrast from the sRGB relative-luminance formula. ## Overall Impression

The craft here is real and it is unevenly distributed. The accessibility engineering is better than most production sites ship: keyboard parity wired into the avatar mechanism, a live-region solution for animated text that almost everyone gets wrong, a double focus ring engineered to survive both the light page and the blue header. The clipboard modal is genuinely beautiful.

And then the page those things live on is a stock three-card grid that could belong to anything.

That is the answer to the complaint that started this: the frontend does not look generic because the tokens are weak or the taste is missing. It looks generic **because the homepage was built as a router and the rest of the site was built as a place.** The design language exists, is documented, and is used everywhere except the front door.

The single biggest opportunity: give the homepage an object. The other pages are a filing cabinet, a bulletin board and a photo wall. The homepage is "three cards," which is not an answer.

## What's Working

**Keyboard parity is designed in, not retrofitted.** `onFocus`/`onBlur` mirror `onMouseEnter`/`onMouseLeave` on every card, so the avatar's arm and the speech bubble respond to Tab exactly as to a mouse. The near-universal pattern is a mascot wired to `:hover` only — decoration that silently excludes keyboard users from a mechanism everyone else gets.

**The animated-text accessibility solution is correct and rare.** `BurbujaDialogo` renders the full string once in an `aria-live="polite" sr-only` layer and the typing string in an `aria-hidden` layer. Almost everyone who builds a typewriter effect ships a live region that re-announces the whole sentence on every character.

**The modal refuses to punish refusal.** Escape and backdrop click both resolve to a real recorded answer rather than reopening the prompt. Combined with the 30-day expiry and never requesting a name or email, this is a principled treatment of a pattern that is almost always dark.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The homepage speaks none of the site's own design language
**Why it matters:** the metaphor that gives this product its coherence is established at the door (the clipboard) and inside the rooms (folder tabs, pinned photo wall), then abandoned in the hallway between them. Every visitor arrives in the one room with no character, and that is the room that forms the impression.
**Fix:** give the homepage an object. The three sections already *are* a filing cabinet, a bulletin board and a photo wall — draw them as such, reusing the `rotate` + pushpin vocabulary already implemented in `/consejered`. The tokens and patterns exist; this is composition work, not new engineering.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable bolder`

### [P1] The most useful content on the page is inert and will clip real Spanish
**Why it matters:** the `<a>` closes before the preview list — `VistaPrevia` sits outside the link. A student who reads a question in the turquoise box taps it and nothing happens. Every `<li>` carries `truncate`: at 360px that box is ~288px at 14.9px, roughly **34 characters**. Lorem fits; real Spanish does not — "¿Qué documentos necesito para solicitar la beca Pell?" clips mid-word. The Noticias rows are worse: the date and em-dash eat ~9 characters, leaving ~25 for a headline. The one idea that makes this homepage feel alive is currently unclickable and will be unreadable the week real content arrives.
**Fix:** make each preview row its own `<Link>` to that specific guide or announcement. Replace `truncate` with `line-clamp-2`, drop to two items per section, and wrap card and preview in one container so the hover lift moves them together instead of pulling them apart.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

### [P1] There is no empty state, and the product guarantees one
**Why it matters:** `obtenerNoticias()` returning `[]` renders an empty `<ul>` inside a tinted box — a ~26px pink rectangle containing nothing. PRODUCT principle 5 is "content goes stale before it goes missing… design for expiry and graceful emptiness," and announcements retire themselves by design. This is not an edge case; it is next July, on purpose.
**Fix:** every `VistaPrevia` branch needs a zero-length case with real copy — "No hay anuncios esta semana. Aquí aparecen cuando los hay." — and an empty section should visually recede rather than render a coloured void. Add an error boundary around the three fetches.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable onboard`

### [P1] The first screen is spent on decoration, and the search box is one level down
**Why it matters:** above the first card title a phone renders roughly 460px of header, a duplicated wordmark, two bracketed placeholders and a 176px avatar that on touch never poses and never points. On a cheap Android with browser chrome that is at or below the fold. The primary user has thirty seconds and one specific question — and the search field that answers it exists on `/guias`, not here.
**Fix:** put the search box on the homepage, under the title, above the fold. Cut the `<h1>` duplication of the header wordmark. Make the largest text a sentence that earns its size. Shrink the avatar on mobile or move it beside the title.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

### [P2] The header violates the project's own contrast rule, and the role chip lies about what it does
**Why it matters:** white on `azul-500` measures **4.43:1**. AA needs 4.5. The site header ships it in three places — the wordmark, the Modo Calma label and the role chip — all at 14–17px, so the 4.5 threshold applies. `app/globals.css` states the rule at lines 13–16 and names this exact colour as the exception. The chips add white on `bg-white/10` at 3.70:1 with `border-white/35` borders at 1.80:1 against the header, which `CLAUDE.md` bans by name. Separately, `ChipRol` renders a `⌄` chevron — universal shorthand for a dropdown — and clicking it wipes the stored role and re-throws the modal.
**Fix:** move the header to `azul-700` (6.39:1), or keep `azul-500` and drop the chips to solid `azul-900` pills with full-opacity borders. Replace the chevron with an explicit "Cambiar", or make it a real two-option switch — which would also, for the first time, let the visitor see what the lens does.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

## Persona Red Flags

**Casey (distracted mobile).** The avatar eats 176px of her first screen and does nothing on touch — it cannot pose, cannot point, and has no purpose on the device most visitors use. Nothing sits in the thumb zone; the first tappable destination is at the fold. The modal's escape hatch, "Continuar como invitado", is a ~20px underlined text link — below the 44px target the project itself enforces in `Boton.tsx` — and it is the option a hurried student most wants.

**Riley (stress tester).** Empty `noticias` → an empty pink bar. Long real headlines → mid-word truncation on two of three previews. Sweeping the mouse across all three cards restarts the typewriter three times and flashes the dashed grey placeholder in the gaps, because `activa` resets to `null` on every `onMouseLeave` — `docs/03-diseno.md` §4 explicitly required "skip on repeat hovers; cute once, irritating the fourth time" and that rule was never implemented. `transition-transform` animates the lift but not the shadow, so the shadow snaps while the card eases.

**Sam (screen reader / keyboard).** Tabbing the cards works and moves the arm. But the preview lists sit outside the links, so a tab-only user meets three bare link labels and never reaches the content that would let them choose. No `aria-describedby` ties a card to its bubble. And at desktop widths the visible fallback text instructs them to "Pasa por encima de una tarjeta" — telling a non-mouse user to hover.

**Yamil, 15, anxious, cracked screen, sunlit hallway.** The first thing this site shows him is a **sign-in sheet** asking who is visiting the counselor today — precisely the act he is avoiding, rendered beautifully. Past it, nothing says *confidencial*, *gratis*, *no tienes que dar tu nombre*, or *puedes solo pasar*. Card borders are `#e4e8ef` at 1.23:1 against white; in direct sun the only thing he sees is the coloured band, so the page reads as three floating stripes. To learn whether she is even in her office, the answer is a grey link called "Disponibilidad", sixth of seven in the footer.

**Doña Carmen, 63, encargada, from a forwarded WhatsApp link.** Her option — "Soy madre, padre o encargado" — is the secondary, white-outlined button; the student's is the filled primary. The design tells her she is the second-class visitor before she has read a word. Past it: "Sra. [Nombre Apellido]", "Escuela Superior [Nombre]", and a footer reading "Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet". No address, no phone, no confirmation this is her grandson's school. And despite WhatsApp being the stated parent channel, there is no share affordance anywhere on the page.

## Minor Observations

- The `<h1>` repeats the header wordmark ~60px above it. The most prominent text on the page tells the visitor nothing new.
- The bubble is anchored `absolute top-2 left-1/2` and never moves. The arm rotates toward the active card; the bubble does not. Half the mechanism is directional. It also overlaps the avatar's own head.
- The desktop empty-state bubble is `border-dashed` grey — it reads as an unloaded placeholder, not as speech.
- `role="presentation"` and `aria-hidden="true"` on the avatar SVG are redundant; keep one.
- `ChipRol` reserves `w-32` but renders ~188px, so the header still shifts ~60px on hydration.
- The homepage cards duplicate markup `components/ui/Tarjeta.tsx` already provides. Two implementations of one card will drift.
- Footer "Personal" is ambiguous in Spanish — staff, or personal data? — three lines above "Este sitio no recoge datos personales."
- Footer duplicates the three section links the cards already provide, in a flat seven-item row with no grouping.
- Off-target, same system: `/consejered` renders `text-naranja` as an eyebrow — 2.54:1, which `docs/03-diseno.md` explicitly forbids as a text colour.
- No print or QR affordance on the homepage, despite PRODUCT principle 2 naming both as part of the product.
- Modo Calma's homepage effect is three accent bands desaturating plus line-height. On a page this sparse, "baja el volumen" is barely audible.

## Content status — a correction worth recording

Assessment B extracted the verbatim visible text. The homepage is **not** all placeholder: the entire role-portal dialog, all three hover-bubble descriptions, every section title, all seven footer labels and the privacy line are finished Spanish. Six strings are Lorem Ipsum (three guide questions, two news headlines, the footer tagline clause) and three are bracketed placeholders (`Sra. [Nombre Apellido]`, `Escuela Superior [Nombre]` twice).

The distinction matters: **the chrome is finished and the content is not.** That is why the page reads as unfinished to a cold visitor while feeling done to the person who built it.

## Questions to Consider

- **What if the homepage *were* the search box?** "¿Qué necesitas saber?" as the largest thing on the page, with the three sections underneath as what-you-do-when-search-fails. The primary user has thirty seconds and one question, and the search already exists — one level down, where they have to guess to find it.
- **The rest of the site is made of objects. What object is the homepage?** Right now the answer is "three cards," which is not an answer.
- **What does answering "¿Quién nos visita hoy?" actually buy the person answering?** Two cards swap and three sentences reword — a change they can never perceive, because they never see the other version.
- **Is a sign-in sheet the right thing at the door of a counseling office?** It is the best-made component in the product and it dramatises the exact social act the anxious student is avoiding.
- **If the counselor could staple one sentence to the hallway wall, what would it be?** That sentence is missing, and its slot is occupied by "Consejería Escolar" — printed twice, sixty pixels apart.
- **What does this page look like in July**, when every announcement has expired itself exactly as designed?
